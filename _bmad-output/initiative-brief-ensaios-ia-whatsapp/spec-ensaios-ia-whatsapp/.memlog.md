- (direction) The spec covers a WhatsApp-first MVP for AI-generated birthday portraits, acquired through Instagram, operated by two partners, and used to validate demand, perceived quality, and operating capacity within a total R$500 pilot ceiling.
- (capability) CAP-1 intent: An adult prospect can request a birthday portrait in natural WhatsApp messages and understand the offer before Pix. Success: the service uses only the configured catalog and approved FAQ, asks one clarifying question at a time, and invents no price or deadline; target at least 90% of test conversations without menus or commands.
- (capability) CAP-2 intent: An eligible customer can authorize private processing of their own adult photo and use privacy rights. Success: own-photo eligibility is checked, consent is recorded before processing, the approved retention policy is disclosed before collection, and access/deletion requests are routed to a human.
- (capability) CAP-3 intent: A customer can accept a package and pay for one order by Pix. Success: image generation is released only after an authenticated matching payment confirmation; repeated or concurrent payment events create no duplicate charge, order, or generation.
- (capability) CAP-4 intent: A paid customer can receive the requested portrait images in the same WhatsApp conversation. Success: each result is linked to its order, delivery is confirmed by channel state, and failures remain visible for recovery; visual acceptance thresholds remain open.
- (capability) CAP-5 intent: A customer may receive an optional preview before Pix. Success: a partner approves it, cost is reserved within configured limits, and the delivered preview is low-resolution and watermarked; package counting and numeric limits remain open.
- (capability) CAP-6 intent: A customer or system can route a dispute, dissatisfaction, privacy request, or explicit human-service request to the partners. Success: the inbox contains actionable context, automation is suspended during human takeover, explicit human requests are all routed, and first human response may take up to 24 hours without a resolution promise.
- (capability) CAP-7 intent: The partners can run a bounded pilot and evaluate its commercial and operational result. Success: committed, reserved, incurred, and unbilled costs count against R$500; new cost-generating work is blocked if the balance is unknown or insufficient; final pilot metrics and go/no-go thresholds remain open.
- (decision) On 2026-10-09 the user approved the pre-Pix FAQ baseline: birthday-only offer; test prices R$19.90/R$39.90/R$59.90 remain hypotheses until margin validation; delivery estimate follows capacity tests; exact retention remains open before announcement and photo collection; no promise of perfect likeness, adjustment, refund, or resolution deadline.
- (constraint) The MVP offers one individual birthday model and accepts only an adult-owned photo; third-party and minor photos are refused before processing.
- (constraint) Pix status, catalog price, refunds, discounts, preview approval, and permission for extra image generation are controlled by deterministic backend rules or an authorized partner; model text is never authoritative for these actions.
- (constraint) The R$500 ceiling is all-in across advertising and operating costs; unknown, committed, reserved, incurred, and unbilled charges count before authorizing new spend.
- (constraint) Photo retention must be defined and disclosed before collection; exact periods and handling of provider copies remain undecided. Photos are not implicitly authorized for advertising, portfolio, or model training.
- (constraint) No customer-facing price is final until package margin is validated, and no delivery estimate is promised before capacity testing. Current test values are R$19.90, R$39.90, and R$59.90 for 1, 5, and 10 images.
- (constraint) The service does not guarantee perfect likeness or promise adjustment, refund, or resolution time before partner review; first human response may take up to 24 hours.
- (constraint) Preview generation requires manual approval and cost reservation; extra generation after dissatisfaction requires partner authorization.
- (note) Non-goal: portrait themes beyond birthday, group or third-party/minor portraits, non-Pix payment, automated refunds, autonomous dispute resolution, and public or training use of customer photos.
- (note) Non-goal: a separate customer app, required external portal/login/ZIP delivery, broad CRM, marketplace, subscription, or generalized multi-merchant platform.
- (question) What final price, package contents, and margin threshold will be approved before the test packages are advertised?
- (question) What delivery estimate, image-quality acceptance criteria, and retry limit will be used after capacity and quality tests? Does the retry count include the initial attempt?
- (question) What retention periods apply to each data type, how are provider copies handled, and how are access, export, and deletion confirmed?
- (question) What commercial remedy, adjustment count, and review timing apply when a customer dislikes the result, and does an approved preview count as the first package image?
- (question) How will the R$500 budget be allocated and enforced across ads and providers, including unbilled costs, and who can pause each spend source?
- (question) Which WhatsApp account, pricing, consent, template, support-route, and automation-transparency rules apply at launch, and which providers and hosting costs are approved?
- (question) Which methods and thresholds measure demand/conversion, perceived quality, usability, support, CAC, margin, and the partners operating effort/capacity, and what results continue or stop the pilot?
- (question) Are audio messages included in the MVP, and what priority, notification channel, post-delivery contact, and automation-resumption rules will the partners use?

- (event) Self-validation pass 2 passed: the updated PRD and its addendum are adopted companions, preserving journeys, detailed requirements, metrics, privacy rules, and technical proposals for downstream work; the kernel carries the load-bearing intent, capabilities, constraints, and pilot gates.
